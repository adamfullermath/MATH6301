    Worksheet Homework 02
    

    Due: Friday 9th October 2026.
    🔗
    Instructions: Solutions should be written (or typed) clearly and legibly. Homeworks should be written alone without the use of AI.
    🔗

    1.

    Let \(X\) be a non-empty set and let \(\mu^*\) be an outer-measure on \(X\text{.}\) Take a subset \(E \subseteq X\text{.}\) Show that, if \(\mu^*(E) = 0\text{,}\) then \(E\) is \(\mu^*\)-measurable.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Assume \(\mu^*(E)=0\text{.}\) Let \(A\subseteq X\) be any subset. Since \(A\cap E\text{,}\) we have that \(\mu^*(A\cap E)=0\) by the monotonicity of \(\mu^*\text{.}\) We also have that, \(\mu^*(A)\geq \mu^*(A\cap E^c)\text{,}\) again by monotonicity. Thus,
    \begin{align*} \mu^*(A) \amp = \mu^*(A) + \mu^*(A\cap E)\\ \amp \geq \mu^*(A\cap E^c)+ \mu^*(A\cap E). \end{align*}
    It follows that \(\mu^*(A) = \mu^*(A\cap E) + \mu^*(A\cap E^c)\text{,}\) and hence \(E\) is \(\mu^*\)-measurable.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    2.

    Let \(X\) be an uncountable set. Let \(\S\) be the collection of all countable subsets of \(X\text{.}\) Define \(\nu \colon \S \rightarrow [0,\infty]\text{,}\) by \(\nu(A) = 0\) for all \(A \in \S\text{.}\)
    🔗

    (a)

    Show that \(\S\) is a semiring, and that \(\nu\) is a premeasure on \(\S\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Any subset of a countable set is countable, Proposition A.2.6.. Thus, is \(A,B \in \S\) then \(A \cap B \in \S\) and \(A\backslash B \in \S\text{.}\) Hence \(\S\) is a semiring.
    🔗
    If \(\{A_i\}_{i=1}^n\) are disjoint countable sets then
    \begin{equation*} 0 = \nu\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^n A_i\right) = \sum_{i=1}^n 0 = \sum_{i=1}^n\nu(A_i). \end{equation*}
    And if \(A \subseteq \bigcup_{i=1}^\infty A_i\) for a countable family \(\{A_i\}_{i=1}^\infty\) of countable sets, then
    \begin{equation*} 0 = \nu(A) = \sum_{i=1}^n 0 = \sum_{i=1}^\infty \nu(A_i). \end{equation*}
    Hence \(\nu\) is a premeasure on \(\S.\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Let \(\nu^*\) be the outer measure induced by \(\nu\text{.}\) Calculate \(\nu^*(A)\) for each \(A \subseteq X\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Let \(A\subseteq X\) be countable. Then \(\mu^*(A)=0\text{.}\) This can be shown directly. Alternatively, it follows from Theorem 2.3.9.
    🔗
    Suppose now that \(A\subseteq X\) is uncountable. Since a countable union of countable sets is countable, there is no countable collection of sets \(\{A_i\}_{i=1}^\infty\) in \(\S\) such that \(A \subseteq \bigcup_{i=1}^\infty A_i\text{.}\) Thus \(\mu^*(A) = \infty\text{,}\) (see Theorem 2.3.2).
    🔗
    Hence, for \(A \subseteq X\)
    \begin{equation*} \nu^*(A) = \begin{cases}0 \amp A \text{ countable},\\ \infty \amp A \text{ uncountable}.\end{cases} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    Let \(\N\) be the collection of \(\nu^*\)-measurable sets. Give a description of the \(\nu^*\)-measurable subsets.
    🔗
    Solution.
    We will show that \(\N = \P(X)\text{.}\) To this end, fix \(E \subseteq X\text{.}\) Take any \(A \subseteq X\text{.}\) If \(A\) is countable, then both \(A\cap E\) and \(A \cap E^c\) are countable. Thus, \(\nu^*(A)=\nu^*(A\cap E) + \nu^*(A\cap E^c)=0.\) If \(A\) is uncountable, then at least one of \(A \cap E\) and \(A \cap E^c\) is uncountable. Thus,
    \begin{equation*} \nu^*(A) = \infty = \nu^*(A\cap E) + \nu^*(A\cap E^c). \end{equation*}
    Hence \(E\) is measurable, and thus \(\N = \P(X).\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (d)

    Define \(\mu \colon \P(X) \rightarrow [0,\infty]\) by \(\mu(A) = 0\) for all \(A \subseteq X\text{.}\) Show that \(\mu\) is a measure on \((X, \P(X))\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    There isn’t much to show here. \(\mu(\emptyset) = 0\text{,}\) and
    \begin{equation*} \mu\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^\infty A_i\right) = \sum_{i=1}^\infty \mu(A_i)=0 \end{equation*}
    for any countable collection of sets \(\{A_i\}_{i=1}^\infty\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (e)

    Let \(\overline{\nu}\) be the Carathéodory extension of \(\nu\) (i.e. \(\overline{\nu}\) is the restriction of \(\nu^*\) to the \(\nu^*\)-measurable sets).
    🔗
    🔗

    (f)

    Note that \(\mu\) is also an extension of \(\S\text{.}\) Why does this not contradict the Carathéodory-Hahn Theorem?
    🔗
    Solution.
    The Carathéodory-Hahn Theorem only guarantees uniqueness in the case when the premeasure \(\nu\) is \(\sigma\)-finite. As \(X\) is uncountable, and \(\S\) is the collection of all countable subsets of \(X\text{,}\) \(X\) can not be covered by countably many elements of \(\S\text{.}\) Thus \(\nu\) is not \(\sigma\)-finite.
    🔗
    🔗

    3.

    Let \(C \subseteq [0,1]\) be the Cantor set; let \(U = [0,1]\backslash C\text{;}\) and let \(\phi \colon [0,1] \rightarrow [0,1]\) be the Cantor-Lebesgue function. Let \(\mu\) be the Borel measure on \([0,1]\) induced by \(\phi\text{.}\) I.e. \(\mu((c,d]) = \phi(d) - \phi(c)\) and \(\mu(\{0\}) = \phi(0).\)
    🔗
    Show that \(\mu([0,1]) = 1\text{,}\) \(\mu(U) = 0\text{,}\) and \(\mu(C) = 1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    By the definition of \(\phi\) (and \(U\)), we can write \(U = \bigcup_{k=1}^\infty I_k\text{,}\) where \(\{I_k\}_{k=1}^\infty\) is a disjoint collection of open intervals, and \(\phi\) is constant on each \(I_k.\) For each \(k\text{,}\) let \(I_k= (a_k,b_k).\)
    🔗
    Fix \(k\text{.}\) As \(\phi\) is continuous, and \(\phi(x)\) is constant for all \(x \in (a_k,b_k)\text{,}\) it follows that \(\phi\) is constant on \([a_k,b_k]\text{.}\) Hence
    \begin{equation*} \mu(I_k) \leq \mu((a_k,b_k]) = \phi(b_k) - \phi(a_k) = 0. \end{equation*}
    Thus, \(\mu(I_k)=0.\) Therefore,
    \begin{equation*} \mu(U)= \sum_{k=1}^\infty \mu(I_k) = 0. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Since \(\phi\) is the cumulative distribution function for \(\mu\) it follows that
    \begin{equation*} \mu([0,1])= \phi(1). \end{equation*}
    By the definition of \(\phi\text{,}\)
    \begin{equation*} \phi(1) = \sup_n\left\{\frac{2^n-1}{2^n} \right\} =1. \end{equation*}
    Hence, \(\mu([0,1])=1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Finally, since \(C \cup U = [0,1]\text{,}\) it follows from the excision property that
    \begin{equation*} \mu(C) = \mu([0,1])-\mu(U)=1. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    4.

    Suppose \(\mu\) and \(\nu\) are measures on a measurable space \((X,\mathcal{F})\) and \(\mu \geq \nu\) (i.e. \(\mu(E) \geq \nu(E)\) for all \(E\in \mathcal{F}\)). Define \(\lambda\) on \(\mathcal{F}\) by
    \begin{equation*} \lambda(E) = \sup\{\mu(F) - \nu(F) \colon F \subseteq E \text{ measurable, } \nu(F)< \infty\}. \end{equation*}
    🔗

    (a)

    Show that \(\lambda\) is a measure on \((X,\mathcal{F})\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    First note that we can’t define \(\lambda(E) = \mu(E) - \nu(E)\) since this value may not be defined (we could be looking at \(\infty - \infty\)).
    🔗
    It is clear that \(\lambda(\emptyset)=0.\) Let \(\{E_k\}_{k=1}^\infty\) be disjoint sets in \(\F\) and let \(E = \bigcup_{k=1}^\infty E_k\text{.}\) Note that if \(F \subseteq E\) is measurable with \(\nu(F)\lt \infty\text{,}\) then \(\{F \cap E_k\}_k\) is a disjoint collection of measurable sets with \(F\cap E_k\subseteq E_k\) and \(\nu(F\cap E_k)\lt\infty.\) However, if \(\{F_k\}_k\) is a collection of (necessarily disjoint) sets with \(F_k \subseteq E_k\) and \(\nu(F_k)\lt \infty\) for each \(k\text{,}\) then it is possible that \(\nu\left(\bigcup_{k=1}^\infty F_k\right)=\infty.\) Hence,
    \begin{align*} \lambda(E) \amp = \sup \left\{\mu(F)-\nu(F) \colon F\subseteq E,\ \nu(F)\lt \infty \right\} \\ \amp = \sup \left\{\mu\left(\bigcup_k F_k\right)-\nu\left(\bigcup_k F_k\right) \colon F_k\subseteq E_k,\ \nu\left(\bigcup_k F_k\right)\lt \infty \right\} \\ \amp \leq \sup \left\{\mu\left(\bigcup_k F_k\right)-\nu\left(\bigcup_k F_k\right) \colon F_k\subseteq E_k,\ \nu(F_k)\lt \infty \right\} \\ \amp = \sup \left\{\sum_{k=1}^\infty[\mu(F_k)-\nu(F_k)] \colon F_k\subseteq E_k,\ \nu(F_k)\lt \infty \right\} \\ \amp = \sum_{k=1}^\infty\sup \left\{\mu(F_k)-\nu(F_k) \colon F_k\subseteq E_k,\ \nu(F_k)\lt \infty \right\}\\ \amp = \sum_{k=1}^\infty \lambda(E_k). \end{align*}
    Note that the penultimate inequality holds since the \(\{F_k\}_{k=1}^\infty\) can be chosen independently of eachother, and all terms are positive (in general the supremum of a sum is less than or equal to the sum of the suprema).
    🔗
    Fix \(n\text{.}\) Note that, a measurable set \(F \subseteq \bigcup_{k=1}^n E_k\) satisfies \(\nu(F) \lt \infty\) if and only if \(\nu(F\cap E_k)\lt \infty\) for each \(k=1,\ldots,n.\) Hence, by similar reasoning as above,
    \begin{equation*} \lambda\left(\bigcup_{k=1}^n E_k\right) = \sum_{k=1}^n \lambda(E_k). \end{equation*}
    Thus,
    \begin{align*} \lambda(E) \amp = \sup \left\{\mu(F)-\nu(F) \colon F\subseteq E,\ \nu(F)\lt \infty \right\} \\ \amp= \left\{\mu\left(\bigcup_k F_k\right)-\nu\left(\bigcup_k F_k\right) \colon F_k\subseteq E_k,\ \nu\left(\bigcup_k F_k\right)\lt \infty \right\} \\ \amp \geq \left\{\mu\left(\bigcup_{k=1}^n F_k\right)-\nu\left(\bigcup_{k=1}^n F_k\right) \colon F_k\subseteq E_k,\ \nu(F_k)\lt \infty \right\} \\ \amp = \sum_{k=1}^n \lambda(E_k). \end{align*}
    As this holds for all \(n\text{,}\) it follows that
    \begin{equation*} \lambda(E) \geq \sum_{k=1}^\infty \lambda(E_k). \end{equation*}
    We have thus shown that \(\lambda(E) = \sum_{k=1}^\infty\lambda(E_k)\text{,}\) and hence \(\lambda\) is a measure.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Show that \(\nu + \lambda = \mu\) (where \(\nu + \lambda\) is defined by \((\nu+\lambda)(E) = \nu(E) + \lambda(E)\) for \(E \in \F\)).
    🔗
    Solution.
    Take \(E \in \F\text{.}\) If \(\nu(E) = \infty\text{,}\) then \(\mu(E)=\infty\) and hence
    \begin{equation*} \infty = \lambda(E) + \nu(E) = \mu(E). \end{equation*}
    Suppose now that \(\nu(E)\lt \infty.\) Then for all measurable \(F \subseteq E\)
    \begin{align*} \mu(E) - \nu(E) \amp= (\mu(F) - \nu(F)) + (\mu(E\backslash F)-\nu(E\backslash F)) \\ \amp\geq \mu(F) - \nu(F). \end{align*}
    Thus \(\lambda(E) = \mu(E) - \nu(E)\text{,}\) and so \(\lambda(E) + \nu(E) = \mu(E).\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    Show that if \((X,\mathcal{F},\nu)\) is \(\sigma\)-finite then the \(\lambda\) is the unique measure satisfying \(\nu + \lambda = \mu\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Hint.
    Assume there are two such measures \(\lambda_1\) and \(\lambda_2\) and show they have to be equal on sets with \(\nu(E)<\infty\text{.}\) Then move to general measurable sets.
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    Let \(\lambda_1\) and \(\lambda_2\) be two measures satisfying
    \begin{equation*} \nu + \lambda_1 = \mu =\nu + \lambda_2. \end{equation*}
    Take \(F\subseteq X\) measurable with \(\nu(F)\lt \infty.\) Then
    \begin{equation*} \nu(F) + \lambda_1(F) = \nu(F) + \lambda_2(F). \end{equation*}
    As \(\nu(F)\lt\infty\text{,}\) it follows that \(\lambda_1(F) = \lambda_2(F).\)
    🔗
    If \((X,\F,\nu)\) is \(\sigma\)-finite, then there are disjoint measurable sets \(\{E_k\}_k\) sets with \(X = \bigcup_{k=1}^\infty E_k\) and \(\nu(E_k)\lt\infty\) for each \(k\text{.}\) Take any measurable \(F \subseteq X\text{.}\) Then, for each \(k\text{,}\) \(\nu(F\cap E_k)\lt \infty\text{,}\) and so
    \begin{align*} \lambda_1(F) \amp= \sum_{k=1}^\infty \lambda_1(F\cap E_k) \\ \amp= \sum_{k=1}^\infty \lambda_2(F\cap E_k) \\ \amp= \lambda_2(F). \end{align*}
    Hence \(\lambda_1 = \lambda_2.\)
    🔗
    Note that, if \((X,\F,\nu)\) is not \(\sigma\)-finite, then Task 4.c does not necessarily hold. For example, let \(\nu\) be the measure defined by
    \begin{equation*} \nu(E) = \begin{cases}0 & \text{if }E = \emptyset\\ \infty & \text{if }E \neq \emptyset,\end{cases} \end{equation*}
    and let \(\mu = \nu\text{.}\) Then, if \(\lambda\) is any measure on \((X,\F)\text{,}\) we have that
    \begin{equation*} \nu + \lambda = \mu. \end{equation*}
