Task 4.c.

Show that if \((X,\mathcal{F},\nu)\) is \(\sigma\)-finite then the \(\lambda\) is the unique measure satisfying \(\nu + \lambda = \mu\text{.}\)
🔗
Hint.
Assume there are two such measures \(\lambda_1\) and \(\lambda_2\) and show they have to be equal on sets with \(\nu(E)<\infty\text{.}\) Then move to general measurable sets.
🔗
🔗
Solution.
Let \(\lambda_1\) and \(\lambda_2\) be two measures satisfying
\begin{equation*} \nu + \lambda_1 = \mu =\nu + \lambda_2. \end{equation*}
Take \(F\subseteq X\) measurable with \(\nu(F)\lt \infty.\) Then
\begin{equation*} \nu(F) + \lambda_1(F) = \nu(F) + \lambda_2(F). \end{equation*}
As \(\nu(F)\lt\infty\text{,}\) it follows that \(\lambda_1(F) = \lambda_2(F).\)
🔗
If \((X,\F,\nu)\) is \(\sigma\)-finite, then there are disjoint measurable sets \(\{E_k\}_k\) sets with \(X = \bigcup_{k=1}^\infty E_k\) and \(\nu(E_k)\lt\infty\) for each \(k\text{.}\) Take any measurable \(F \subseteq X\text{.}\) Then, for each \(k\text{,}\) \(\nu(F\cap E_k)\lt \infty\text{,}\) and so
\begin{align*} \lambda_1(F) \amp= \sum_{k=1}^\infty \lambda_1(F\cap E_k) \\ \amp= \sum_{k=1}^\infty \lambda_2(F\cap E_k) \\ \amp= \lambda_2(F). \end{align*}
Hence \(\lambda_1 = \lambda_2.\)
🔗
Note that, if \((X,\F,\nu)\) is not \(\sigma\)-finite, then Task 4.c does not necessarily hold. For example, let \(\nu\) be the measure defined by
\begin{equation*} \nu(E) = \begin{cases}0 & \text{if }E = \emptyset\\ \infty & \text{if }E \neq \emptyset,\end{cases} \end{equation*}
and let \(\mu = \nu\text{.}\) Then, if \(\lambda\) is any measure on \((X,\F)\text{,}\) we have that
\begin{equation*} \nu + \lambda = \mu. \end{equation*}
